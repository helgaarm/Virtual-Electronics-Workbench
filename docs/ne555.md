# NE555N model and sources

## Implemented device

`NE555N` is a visible electronic device in a reusable `DIP-8` physical package. Circuit extraction maps its eight breadboard pins into a reusable internal electrical subcircuit. The internal model is an original educational hybrid analogue model, not copied manufacturer netlist data and not a transistor-for-transistor reconstruction of proprietary silicon.

The model contains:

- a physical three-resistor 5 kohm divider that creates the lower and control-voltage reference nodes;
- smooth analogue comparator current stages;
- a cross-coupled saturating analogue latch;
- an electrical RESET comparison and priority path;
- finite-resistance high/low output stages;
- a finite-resistance discharge path.

The smooth stages use the generic nonlinear Jacobian interface. There is no global NE555 solver branch, hard threshold statement, oscillator equation, or display-only waveform. In the astable starter, the external RA/RB/C network charges and discharges through the simulated pin network, and the oscilloscope reads accepted solver samples from pins 3 and 2/6.

## Supported behavior

- standard pin mapping: 1 GND, 2 TRIGGER, 3 OUTPUT, 4 RESET, 5 CONTROL, 6 THRESHOLD, 7 DISCHARGE, 8 VCC;
- nominal 4.5–16 V supply metadata;
- lower/upper comparison around the divider's approximately one-third/two-thirds supply references;
- latch memory, electrical RESET override, CONTROL influence, output loading, and discharge switching;
- natural astable oscillation, timing-node cycling, frequency change when RB changes, and stop under RESET.
- automated astable operation at both 4.5 V and 16 V supply limits.

The starter uses RA = 10 kohm, RB = 10 kohm, and C = 10 microfarad. The traditional ideal astable estimate is:

`f = 1 / (0.693 × (RA + 2RB) × C) ≈ 4.81 Hz`

This formula is a comparison only; it is not used by the solver. The extracted 5 V starter regression accepts 4.5–5.5 Hz, a 55–80% high duty fraction, output below 0.5 V and above 4 V, and a timing-node excursion below 2 V and above 3 V. The current nominal result is about 4.9 Hz. The educational hybrid model's finite transition widths and output/discharge resistances cause expected differences in frequency, duty cycle, voltage swing, and startup behavior.

## Original macro parameters

These values are independently chosen educational tuning parameters, not copied or guaranteed NE555 production limits:

| Model element | Value | Provenance |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Reference divider | 5 kohm / 5 kohm / 5 kohm | source-derived classic bipolar-555 topology/value |
| Comparator assertion pull-up / maximum sink | 100 kohm / 0.45 mA | tuned to preserve switching across 4.5–16 V |
| Comparator transition width | 40 mV (60 mV for switches by default) | tuned differentiable approximation |
| Latch pull-ups / cross sinks | 5 kohm / 10 mA, 80 mV transition | tuned analogue-memory approximation |
| Lumped latch-node capacitance | 10 pF per latch node | plausible on-die parasitic charge and state continuity across breadboard topology changes |
| Set/threshold-reset/RESET priority paths | 50/15/100 mA sinks and 20/120/5 ohm pull-up switches | tuned electrical priority approximation |
| RESET reference | 47 kohm bias resistor plus a diode junction | tuned to approximately 0.6–0.7 V across the supply range |
| Output high / low resistance | 50 ohm / 20 ohm | tuned finite-drive approximation |
| Discharge on resistance | 30 ohm | tuned finite-drive approximation |

## Known limitations

- The NE555 subcircuit is hybrid analogue, not a full BJT-level die model. The current model's comparator/latch/output stages are differentiable macro primitives.
- Supply current is not calibrated to a specific production lot and visible-component aggregate supply-current telemetry is not yet exposed.
- Propagation delay, temperature drift, output saturation curves, leakage, noise, current limiting, semiconductor charge storage, and package parasitics are simplified or absent.
- A fixed timestep must be small enough to resolve the requested oscillator frequency; very stiff circuits can still return an explicit convergence failure.
- The starter defaults to a 1 ms step. Its synchronous dense-matrix solve is suitable for this small example but can consume a substantial share of the browser main thread at higher speed settings; worker execution and sparse/cached matrix structures are future optimizations.
- The NE555 monostable starter and a faster optional behavioral model are follow-up work.
